## Four reusable interactions

| Type | Use when | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Multiple choice | Recognizing a name, term, category, or short answer | Tap one option, then Check. Prefer 1–4 words where meaning permits; names and a few terms may be longer. |
| Word-bank blank | Recalling a missing word or meaningful phrase | Tap or drag a tile into a blank; tap it to return it. Preserve multiword phrases as single tiles. Check after all blanks are filled. |
| Match pairs | Connecting two or three distinct relationships | Tap an item, then its partner. Support text, photos, and stickers. Immediate pair feedback; Continue after all pairs are connected. |
| Put in order | Following or recalling an explicitly taught sequence, route, or progression | Reorder cards by drag, tapping positions, or accessible move controls. Check the complete sequence. |

No select-two or sorting component is needed. Those original questions are converted to single-choice, word-bank blanks, or ordering without changing the underlying facts. Do not force all four types into every lesson.

### Word-bank rules

- Each listed word or phrase is one tile; a full name stays on one tile.
- One-blank questions use one correct tile plus distractors.
- Two-blank questions explicitly mark either fixed slot order or interchangeable correct tiles. For “A and B” lists with equivalent slots, accept both correct orders. Do not mark an equivalent answer wrong because the two tiles are swapped.
- Tiles already placed disappear from the bank, leaving an optional placeholder. Return them when removed.
- Shuffle initial banks. Do not reveal answer order through bank position, color, or tile animation.

### Matching pictures and stickers

Use the per-question visual direction below. Pictures are intentional, not mandatory for every concept. Photo + visible name cards reinforce the visual while preserving the original name-to-meaning test. Abstract historical roles remain text-based.

- Show each visual with its name in the preceding teaching content before using it in a matching task. This is a presentation asset addition, not a new fact.
- Actual landmark photos should be specific and identifiable; use simple stickers for clear objects such as a train, food bowl, or Mount Fuji.
- Do not use generic city stickers to distinguish Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka.
- Do not rely on appearance alone to classify every religious site or distinguish geiko from maiko.
- Each right-hand meaning must map uniquely within that set. Do not duplicate “Buddhist temple” as two different answers in the same round.
- Prefer 2–3 pairs. Split the five-item Shinkansen review into two rounds within one question.
- Correct pairs stay visible but subdued. A mismatch resets the attempted pair without erasing completed ones. Never reshuffle during an active round.
- Add accessible image descriptions and keyboard operation. Assets are requirements here, not generated or bundled images.
